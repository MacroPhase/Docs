# Understanding Macros

You've probably heard someone say "carbs are bad" or "you need to eat more protein." Both statements are partly true, partly wrong, and missing the actual context.

Here's what actually matters about macronutrients — and what doesn't.

## Protein — The One That Matters Most

Protein builds muscle, preserves it during a deficit, and keeps you fuller longer than any other macro. It also costs more energy to digest — your body burns 20-30% of protein calories just processing it.

### How Much?

- General health: 0.8-1.0 g/kg
- Fat loss: 1.6-2.2 g/kg
- Muscle building: 1.6-2.4 g/kg

> **Example:** A 90 kg person cutting weight would typically aim for roughly 145-200g of protein per day. That's about 580-800 calories from protein alone.

> 📊 **In MacroPhase**
> MacroPhase defaults to a moderate protein tier (1.6 g/kg) and lets you adjust. You can choose Low, Moderate, High, or Extra High, or set a fixed gram target.

> ⚠️ **Common Myth**
> More protein is always better. Going from 60g to 140g per day has a huge impact. Going from 220g to 300g usually won't. There's a ceiling, and most people are already near it if they're eating a reasonable amount of meat, fish, eggs, or dairy.

> **Protein's Scorecard:**
> - Satiety: ★★★★☆
> - Muscle retention: ★★★★★
> - Thermic effect: ★★★★☆
> - Recovery: ★★★★☆

*Sources: Helms et al., 2014, JISSN; Morton et al., 2018, Br J Sports Med*

## Fat — The One Everyone Fears Wrongly

Dietary fat doesn't make you fat. It supports hormone production (including testosterone and estrogen), helps absorb vitamins A, D, E, and K, and keeps your brain functioning — which is literally 60% fat by weight.

### How Much?

- Minimum threshold: ~20% of calories (below this, hormones can suffer)
- Most people do well at 25-35%
- Keto dieters: 70-80% (works for some, not for all)

> **Example:** On a 2,000 cal/day diet, 30% from fat = 600 cal = ~67g of fat per day.

> ⚠️ **Common Myth**
> Eating fat does not directly become body fat. Body fat gain is driven by sustained calorie surplus. You can eat a high-fat diet and lose weight, or eat a low-fat diet and gain weight. The total calorie balance is what determines fat gain or loss, not the percentage of fat in your diet.

> **Fat's Scorecard:**
> - Satiety: ★★★☆☆
> - Hormone support: ★★★★★
> - Vitamin absorption: ★★★★★
> - Long-term health: ★★★★☆

## Carbs — The Unnecessary Villain

Carbs are your body's preferred energy source. They fuel high-intensity exercise, support brain function (your brain uses ~120g of glucose per day), and help with recovery after workouts.

### How Much?

Carbs fill whatever's left after protein and fat are set. For most people eating a balanced diet, carbs end up being the largest portion of calories.

- Sedentary: 0.5-1.0 g/kg
- Moderate exercise: 3-5 g/kg
- High-intensity training: 5-8 g/kg

> **Example:** A 90 kg person with moderate activity, eating 2,400 cal/day, with 180g protein and 70g fat, would have roughly 350g of carbs remaining.

> ⚠️ **Common Myth**
> Carbs do not automatically cause fat gain. If calories are controlled, high-carb diets are just as effective for fat loss as low-carb diets. The popularity of low-carb diets comes from the fact that reducing carbs often reduces total calories — but it's the calorie reduction doing the work, not the carb reduction.

> **Carbs' Scorecard:**
> - Satiety: ★★☆☆☆
> - Exercise performance: ★★★★★
> - Glycogen replenishment: ★★★★★
> - Mood and sleep: ★★★★☆

## How MacroPhase Sets Your Split

3. **Carbs** as the remainder

You can override any of these manually. The app ensures the three macros sum to your total calorie target.

## Alcohol and Fiber

**Alcohol** provides 7 calories per gram but isn't a macronutrient. It doesn't build muscle, has minimal nutritional value, and impairs recovery. MacroPhase tracks it separately.

**Fiber** is a type of carbohydrate your body can't fully digest. It supports digestive health, blood sugar regulation, and satiety. Aim for 25-35g per day. MacroPhase tracks it as a micronutrient.

## The Bottom Line

Most people obsess over macro ratios when total calories and protein intake matter far more. Hit your calorie target, prioritize protein, eat enough fat to stay healthy, and fill the rest with carbs. If you're doing those three things, the exact ratio matters less than consistency.

> 💡 **Key Idea**
> If you remember one thing from this article: calories determine whether you gain or lose weight. Protein determines how much of that weight is muscle vs fat. The carb-to-fat ratio matters less than either.
